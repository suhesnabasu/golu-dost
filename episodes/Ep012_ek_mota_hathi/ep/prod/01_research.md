## 1. What parents search right now (late Nov / early winter)

- **Evergreen head terms stay strongest:** "hindi rhymes for kids", "hindi balgeet", "बच्चों की कविता", "hindi poem for kids", "nursery rhymes for kids", "baby songs hindi". The biggest Hindi-kids channels (Kids Channel India, ChuChu-style, Luke & Lily India, Baby Toot Toot Hindi, HooplaKidz Hindi, Bubbly TV, Chote Sitare) all title around "Hindi Rhymes / Baby Songs / Balgeet", and their top videos are classic rhymes with animal heroes. Elephant (हाथी) rhymes come up again and again (vidIQ channel stat pages; many "Ek Mota Hathi" uploads on Amazon Music, Last.fm, Dailymotion).
- **"Ek Mota Hathi" is a known, searched title.** Several unrelated channels and labels have their own versions (Ladoo Kids "Ek Mota Hathi Ghumne Chala", Palak Gujela "Ek Mota Hathi", LooLoo "Popular Hindi Kids Rhymes"). So people really search this title, and no single owner shows up.
- **Winter context:** in Nov–Dec, parents watch more cosy, indoor, "cold weather" content (woolly clothes, scarves, stars at night). That helps candidate #2 (Baa Baa Black Sheep and wool) and fits Golu's orange scarf.
- **2026 platform climate (important for monetization):** NYT (26 Feb 2026, republished in The Nightly, 20 Mar 2026) reported YouTube removing low-quality or garbled AI kids videos and taking channels out of YPP. Experts favour a **clear beginning, middle and end, simple language, intentional repetition and paced delivery**. Kids-animation trend pieces (Whizzy Studios 2026) say **character-led 3D Shorts** grow fastest, and a plain nursery rhyme alone is no longer enough. Golu's twist should be a small story beat, so the video isn't just a karaoke rhyme.

## 3. Rights check: "एक मोटा हाथी / One Elephant Went Out to Play"

### English version: CONFIRMED traditional
- traditionalmusic.co.uk (Folk & Traditional Song Lyrics) lists it as a **children's singing game collected in 1959 from a 16-year-old girl in Motherwell, Lanarkshire, Scotland**. Children join one by one, each holding the waist of the child in front, lurching like an elephant. It also lists related versions: Edith Fowke, *Songs and Games of Seven Nations* (1969, "One little elephant balancing", Toronto), and Iona & Peter Opie, *The Singing Game* (1985, tune from Manchester 1975, "foreign versions" noted). **No author or composer is listed.**
- Sharon, Lois & Bram popularised it (*The Elephant Show*; picture book *Sharon, Lois and Bram's One Elephant Went Out to Play*). Their **recording, arrangement and book are copyrighted, but the song itself is credited as traditional.** Raffi songbook and many school/early-years resources (Bolton Start Well song card 9, NDNA, TES, U. Arkansas "Favorite Songs & Fingerplays", makingmusicfun) print it without an author.
- **What this means for us:** use the folk words with **our own melody and arrangement**. Don't imitate the Sharon, Lois & Bram tune, the "Elephant Show" branding or the book art.

### Hindi version: ANONYMOUS folk adaptation, no single owner found
- The text people recognise ("एक मोटा हाथी झूम के चला / मकड़ी के जाले में जाके फँसा …") is clearly an Indian school-rhyme adaptation of the English game. It circulates in **many different wordings** ("घूमने चला", "झूम के चला", "फँसा", "दूसरे हाथी को बुलाओ भाई") across unrelated channels (Ladoo Kids, Palak Gujela, LooLoo, many more). None of them credits a lyricist, and I found no poet, film or label that claims it. Searches on Rekhta, Hindwi, UT Austin and Columbia STARTALK found no attribution.
- **Residual risk:** any one channel's *exact* wording, and certainly its melody, could be claimed as that channel's arrangement (Content ID on audio). **Fix:** we use **our own Hindi wording**, a direct translation of the PD English text plus the common folk opening "एक मोटा हाथी झूम के चला", which appears in every version and belongs to nobody. We also use our own tune, and never upload a reference track.
- **Verdict: SAFE to use on these terms.** Our own Hindi rendering, our own melody, the traditional English text.

### Content note
The traditional Hindi has "जाले में **फँसा**" (got trapped/stuck). For ages 2–8 we change this to "**झूला झूला**" (swung on it), which matches the English "had such enormous fun". The spider is a tiny, smiling, toy-like friend who *invites* them. No creepy legs, no dark corners.

## 4. Three ranked episode ideas

### #1 (RECOMMENDED): "एक मोटा हाथी / One Elephant Went Out to Play"
- **Set:** park playground (reuse). The "spider web" is a **rope climbing-net / web swing** with a tiny friendly spider (Makdi) sitting at the top. This makes the impossible image (elephants on a web) look playful and safe.
- **Lyrics (our rendering; 2 short verses, ~8 bars each):**

**Verse 1 (Hindi)**
> एक मोटा हाथी, झूम के चला,
> मकड़ी के जाले पे झूला झूला।
> इतना मज़ा आया, इतना मज़ा,
> दूजे हाथी को बुलाया: "आजा!"

**Verse 2 (English, traditional text plus the Golu twist)**
> Two elephants went out to play,
> Upon a spider's web one day.
> They had such enormous fun,
> They called for… **GOLU** to come!

- **Golu twist (bilingual):** Golu is the *baby* elephant who's been watching. He's "elephant number THREE!" and runs in with his orange scarf flapping. He jumps on, and the web goes **BOING!** All three bounce high and land giggling in the (reused) leaf pile. The tiny spider pops up and laughs: "तीन! Three!" Payoff: Golu is little, but he made the biggest bounce. That's character-led, not just karaoke.
- **Hook (0–1.5 s):** Golu's face fills the frame, eyes wide, trunk pointing up at a giant shimmering web. He whispers "हाथी… जाले पे?!" (an elephant… on a web?!), then a "boing" sound and a cut to elephant #1 bouncing. It's an impossible, funny image that makes kids stop scrolling.
- **Learning goal:** counting 1→2→3 in Hindi and English (एक/One, दो/Two, तीन/Three), plus cumulative "add one more" (early addition), plus inviting friends to play (sharing fun).
- **Why it retains / gets rewatched:** a cumulative pattern kids can predict, a strong "boing" payoff at the end, Golu IS the elephant (strong brand fit), the counting number pops on screen every time, and a loop ending (spider holds up 1 finger again as the last frame, so the replay feels seamless).
- **Title / keyword ideas:**
  - Title: `एक मोटा हाथी 🐘 One Elephant Went Out to Play | Hindi + English Rhyme | Golu & Dost #shorts`
  - Hindi: एक मोटा हाथी, हाथी वाली कविता, बच्चों की कविता, हिंदी बालगीत, गिनती गीत, हाथी गाना
  - Hinglish: ek mota hathi, ek mota hathi jhoom ke chala, hathi wali poem, hindi rhymes for kids, hindi balgeet, bacchon ki kavita, counting rhyme hindi
  - English: one elephant went out to play, elephant song for kids, counting song, nursery rhymes for kids, baby elephant cartoon, spider web song
- **Risks:** (a) Hindi wording: keep ours, don't copy any channel's text or melody. (b) Elephants are heavy, and toddlers may copy the bouncing. The net is a playground toy at ground level with a soft leaf landing, so no falls from height. (c) Spider phobia: keep it tiny, round, pastel and smiling, and never show close-up legs.

### #2: "Baa Baa Black Sheep / बा बा भेड़ (winter scarf)"
- **PD evidence:** printed in *Tommy Thumb's Pretty Song Book* (London, c. 1744), anonymous, centuries in the public domain. The Hindi text would be our own translation.
- **Lyrics:**

**Hindi (our translation)**
> बा-बा काली भेड़, ऊन है क्या?
> हाँ जी, हाँ जी, तीन बोरी भरा!

**English (traditional, twist in last line)**
> Baa, baa, black sheep, have you any wool?
> Yes sir, yes sir, three bags full!
> One for the master, one for the dame,
> And one for **GOLU**, his scarf to make!

- **Golu twist:** it's a cold morning in the winter park. Golu is shivering, the sheep knits him the orange scarf, and his signature look gets an origin story.
- **Hook:** Golu sneezes in the cold ("आ-छू!"), then cut to a fluffy sheep.
- **Learning goal:** counting to 3 (bags), plus winter = warm clothes, plus saying thank you.
- **Keywords:** baa baa black sheep hindi, बा बा ब्लैक शीप, winter song for kids, sardi ki kavita, hindi rhymes.
- **Risks:** very saturated English term. Overlaps lightly with the done "Golu's big sneeze" episode, so use a different hook if picked. The modern debate about the rhyme's history is minor, and the "master/dame" words are archaic.

### #3: "आलू कचालू बेटा कहाँ गए थे"
- **PD evidence:** an anonymous Hindi folk rhyme, listed without an author in UT Austin's Hindi nursery-rhyme collection and sung all over North India.
- **Traditional text (ours softened):**

> आलू कचालू बेटा कहाँ गए थे?
> बैंगन की टोकरी में सो रहे थे।
> ~~बैंगन ने लात मारी, रो रहे थे~~ → **बैंगन ने गुदगुदी की, हँस रहे थे!**

**English twist**
> Aloo Kachaloo, where did you go?
> Sleeping in the veggie basket, in a row!
> Golu found him, tickle-tickle, *ho ho ho!*

- **Set:** kitchen (reuse). Golu looks for the missing potato. It's a hide-and-seek veggie gag.
- **Learning goal:** vegetable names (आलू, बैंगन, टमाटर), "where?" questions.
- **Keywords:** aloo kachaloo beta kahan gaye the, आलू कचालू, hindi rhymes for kids, sabziyon ki kavita.
- **Risks:** we must change the traditional kick/crying line, and some parents notice changes. Overlaps lightly with the done "kitchen shapes" set and "guess the fruit". Hindi-only, so the English half is weaker.

## 5. Final recommendation
**Go with #1 "एक मोटा हाथी / One Elephant Went Out to Play."** The leaning is **confirmed**:
- **English:** documented folk singing game (collected 1959 in Scotland, in Fowke 1969 and Opie 1985), no author. Only Sharon, Lois & Bram's recording, arrangement and book are protected, and we use none of them.
- **Hindi:** an anonymous school/folk adaptation in many variants. No poet, film or label found. To be safe we use **our own wording** (above) and **our own melody**.
- It fits perfectly: Golu IS an elephant, it adds counting 1–3 without repeating the done 1–5/1–10 episodes (it teaches "add one more"), it reuses the park set and leaf pile, and the "Golu is number three, BOING" twist makes it a story rather than a plain rhyme.
